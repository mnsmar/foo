## Overview

Aging is accompanied by widespread disruption of RNA homeostasis. Endogenously generated
RNA fragments constitute a significant fraction of the total RNA pool in cells, and
dynamically change in response to cellular stress. We have found extensive RNA remodeling
in the aging brain that affects nearly all aspects of RNA homeostasis and exerts global
effects on brain function.

## Approach

We combine long-read direct RNA sequencing, metabolic RNA labeling, and computational
modeling to profile RNA stability, processing, and decay across cell types and tissues
at different ages. This allows us to identify specific RNA regulatory events that drive
or protect against aging-associated decline.

## Key Findings

- Pervasive RNA isoform remodeling in the aging mouse brain, with cell-type specificity
- Stress granules as hubs for selective mRNA decay during cellular stress
- Senescence suppresses the integrated stress response and activates a distinct secretory program
