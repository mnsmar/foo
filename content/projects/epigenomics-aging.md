## Overview

Epigenetic changes are a hallmark of aging. We study how specific epigenetic marks —
particularly DNA hydroxymethylation (5hmC) — shape the transcriptional landscape of
aging cells and tissues. Our work revealed that gene body 5hmC acts as a regulatory
brake, restricting the magnitude of transcriptional changes that accumulate with age.

## Approach

We apply genome-wide epigenetic profiling alongside transcriptomics across ages to
identify causal relationships between epigenetic remodeling and gene expression dysregulation.
Machine learning models are used to predict and interpret the functional consequences
of age-associated epigenetic changes.

## Key Findings

- Gene body DNA hydroxymethylation restricts the magnitude of transcriptional changes
  during aging, revealing a novel epigenetic buffering mechanism
